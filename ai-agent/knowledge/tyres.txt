# PIRELLI FORMULA 1 TYRES AND REGULATIONS

## Tyre Supplier
Pirelli is the sole official tyre supplier for Formula 1. F1 uses 18-inch low-profile wheels and tyres.

## Slick (Dry-Weather) Compounds
Pirelli manufactures five to six slick compounds numbered from C0 to C5 (with C1 being the hardest and C5/C6 being the softest):
- Hard Compound (White Sidewall): Slowest warm-up time, lowest peak grip, but longest lifespan and highest resistance to degradation. Ideal for abrasive tracks and hot ambient temperatures.
- Medium Compound (Yellow Sidewall): The balanced, versatile compound offering a compromise between speed and endurance.
- Soft Compound (Red Sidewall): Maximum grip and fastest lap time, rapid warm-up, but prone to thermal degradation and graining over longer stints. Used primarily for qualifying and aggressive race strategies.

For each race weekend, Pirelli selects three distinct compounds from their range and labels them White (Hard), Yellow (Medium), and Red (Soft).

## Wet-Weather Compounds
- Intermediate (Green Sidewall): Designed for damp, standing-water-free, or drying tracks. Grooved tread displaces approx. 30 litres of water per second per tyre at 300 km/h.
- Full Wet (Blue Sidewall): Deeply grooved tread for heavy rainfall and severe standing water. Displaces approx. 85 litres of water per second per tyre at 300 km/h to prevent aquaplaning.

## Mandatory Race Tyre Rules
1. Mandatory Two-Compound Rule: In a dry race, each driver MUST use at least two different dry slick compounds (e.g., Medium to Hard, Soft to Hard). This makes at least one pit stop mandatory.
2. Wet Race Exemption: If Intermediate or Full Wet tyres are used at any point during a race, the mandatory two-compound rule is waived, and drivers are not forced to make a pit stop (though usually they do).
3. Weekend Tyre Allocation: Each driver receives 13 sets of dry tyres (usually 8 Soft, 3 Medium, 2 Hard), 4 sets of Intermediates, and 3 sets of Wets per race weekend.

## Tyre Degradation and Performance Factors
- Thermal Degradation: Overheating tyre surface and carcass causing loss of grip.
- Graining: Small rubber bits tearing from the tread and sticking to the surface, reducing surface contact.
- Blistering: Excessive core heat causing rubber below the surface to vaporize and break away.
- Tyre Blankets: Pre-heating tyres in the pit garage (currently limited to 70°C for slicks) to ensure immediate operating temperature upon exiting the pits.
